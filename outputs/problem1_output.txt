AMAPE MANAGEMENT SUMMARY - Problem 1 Data Analysis
======================================================================

To the Management of AMAPE (Acme Medical Analysis and Prediction Enterprises):

Our exploratory data analysis of the heart disease dataset comprising 270 patient records
and 13 medical features reveals several important findings for the development of our predictive
model. The features most strongly correlated with heart disease diagnosis (a1p2) are thal
(r = +0.5250), nmvcf (r = +0.4553), eia (r = +0.4193),
mhr (r = -0.4185), and opst (r = +0.4180), indicating that
these clinical measurements carry the most predictive power for identifying patients at risk of
heart disease. Among inter-feature relationships, opst and dests show the strongest
mutual correlation (r = +0.6097), followed by age and mhr
(r = -0.4022), which suggests some degree of shared variance among certain predictors.

Regarding class balance, our dataset contains 150 patients without heart
disease and 120 with heart disease (balanced at
44.4% minority class), which is favorable for standard classifier training without resampling techniques.

Point-biserial correlation analysis with formal hypothesis testing (alpha = 0.05) confirmed that
11 of 13 features are statistically significant predictors of heart disease:
thal, nmvcf, eia, mhr, opst, cpt, dests, sex, age, rer, rbp. The features sc, fbs showed no statistically significant association with the target variable.

Outlier analysis using the IQR method identified 7 features containing
outliers, notably cpt (7.4%), fbs (14.8%), nmvcf (7.0%). These outliers may affect
distance-based and linear model performance and should be monitored, though tree-based methods
are inherently robust to their influence.

We are highly confident that thal, nmvcf, eia, mhr, opst, cpt will be strong predictors of heart disease,
moderately confident about dests, sex, age, and
rer, rbp, sc, fbs show weak predictive signal that may still contribute
marginally within ensemble models. Given these findings, we expect supervised machine learning
classifiers to achieve good predictive accuracy in the range of 0.78 to 0.95, with non-linear
methods like SVM and Random Forest expected to perform best on this dataset.
