To the Management of AMAPE (Acme Medical Analysis and Prediction Enterprises):

After training and evaluating six supervised machine learning classifiers on our heart disease
dataset using an 80/20 train-test split with standardized features, we recommend Support Vector Machines
as the best-performing model for AMAPE's heart disease prediction application, achieving a test
accuracy of 0.94 and a combined accuracy of 0.92 on
the full dataset. The second-best performer was Random Forest with a test accuracy of
0.93, which also demonstrates strong generalization capability. Among
the six methods evaluated, Decision Tree showed the lowest performance with an accuracy of
0.78, which may be attributed to its sensitivity to hyperparameter constraints and
the complexity of clinical decision boundaries in this dataset.

To ensure the robustness of these findings beyond a single train-test split, we conducted 5-fold
cross-validation with proper pipeline scaling. The cross-validated results confirm K-Nearest Neighbor
as the most reliable classifier with a mean CV accuracy of 0.8444 (+/-0.0569),
providing higher confidence that this performance will generalize to unseen patient data.

ROC analysis further validates our recommendation: Support Vector Machines achieved the highest AUC of
0.978, indicating excellent discrimination between disease and non-disease patients
across all classification thresholds. Learning curve analysis for the top two models shows:
Support Vector Machines (Overfitting (gap > 0.10), gap=0.149); Random Forest (Overfitting (gap > 0.10), gap=0.187), suggesting some overfitting that may be addressed with additional training data.

For K-Nearest Neighbors, systematic evaluation of K from 1 to 30 via cross-validation identified
K=11 as optimal (CV accuracy: 0.8444).

Based on cross-validated performance and AUC analysis, we recommend K-Nearest Neighbor for deployment
in AMAPE's heart disease prediction system, with an expected real-world accuracy of approximately
0.84.
